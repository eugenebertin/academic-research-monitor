  Material mentioned: TeO$_x$:Se
  DOI: (not supplied by arXiv)
  Publication: No matching journal DOI found in Crossref; peer review unverified
  Abstract:
    Tellurium dioxide (TeO$_2$) is a wide-bandgap oxide with attractive processing
    versatility and emerging p-type semiconducting behaviour, making it a promising
    material system for optoelectronic and photovoltaic applications. However,
    TeO$_2$-derived thin films prepared by scalable manufacturing processes, such as
    vacuum sublimation, can contain residual metallic Te$^0$ species that introduce trap
    states, limit hole transport, and promote carrier recombination. Here, we
    demonstrate a selenium alloying strategy to control the defect chemistry of
    vacuum-sublimed TeO$_x$ films. TeO$_2$:Se powder mixtures (90:10~wt\%) were

    optimised conditions. Structural, chemical, optical, and electrical analyses show
    that prolonged annealing promotes Se redistribution, suppresses residual Te$^0$ to
    approximately 0.4\%, reduces the film's surface roughness, and shifts the
    valence-band edge. To evaluate the potential of TeO$_x$:Se for photovoltaic
    applications, we fabricated thin-film solar cells with an
    ITO/ZnO/TeO$_x$:Se/MoO$_x$/Ag architecture. Devices based on an 80~nm TeO$_x$:Se
    active layer annealed for 9~h exhibit optimal performance, with broadband
    photocurrent generation from 300 to 1000~nm, yielding an open-circuit voltage of
    0.50~V, a short-circuit current density of 8.50~mA~cm$^{-2}$, a fill factor of 0.44,
    and a maximum power conversion efficiency of approximately 1.87\%. These results
    establish selenium-alloyed TeO$_x$ as a viable oxide-derived absorber for thin-film
    photovoltaics and highlight chemical-state control as an effective route for
    improving photovoltaic performance.
